Year, month and day calendar

Pick a year, a month and a day and see how many times the Wayback Machine captured a website, on a heat scale. Empty periods are marked and never re-fetched.

WayWayBack talks to the Wayback Machine's own sparkline and calendar-captures endpoints, so it fetches only the year you are looking at instead of crawling the whole index.

Every day cell shows its capture count on a heat scale. Years, months and days with no captures are crossed out and are never requested, which keeps the app fast and gentle on the Internet Archive.

Results are cached on the device, so revisiting a site is instant.

Smart search

Search merges instant URL guesses with optional Wikipedia and npm lookups, and remembers your recent searches. No API keys, no accounts.

Type example.com and WayWayBack immediately offers https://example.com and the www variant. Type a well-known name and the optional Wikipedia engine maps the article to its official homepage.

The optional npm engine maps a package name to its repository or website. Both engines are keyless and can be switched off in Settings.

Recent searches are saved locally and offered next time.

Per-day snapshot list with filters

Every capture of a day with its time and HTTP status. Smart hour, minute and second filters only offer the times that actually exist.

Tap a day to see every snapshot taken on it, with time and HTTP status. Tap a snapshot to open the archived page in your browser.

Busy sites can have hundreds of captures per day. The hour, minute and second filters only list values that exist, so you never pick an empty combination.

Share a single snapshot or the whole day as a web.archive.org link.

Stars and history

Star websites and individual snapshots, and keep a local history of every link and capture you opened. Everything stays on your device.

The home screen has three tabs: starred Websites, starred Snapshots and History. Tabs can be reordered in Settings.

History records every link and capture you opened, with the time, and can be cleared at any time.

All of it is stored locally in an on-device database. Nothing is uploaded.

Share a link to open its archive

Share a link from any Android app, or use a wwb://open?url=… deep link, to open the site's Wayback Machine calendar straight away.

Found a dead link? Use the Android share sheet and pick WayWayBack. The app opens on that site's archive calendar with no copy-paste.

Deep links are supported too: wwb://open?url=https://example.com opens the calendar for example.com.

Themes and layout

Light, dark or system theme, drag-to-reorder home tabs and optional search engines, all in a simple settings screen.

Pick Light, Dark or follow the system. Drag the home tabs into the order you prefer.

Toggle the optional Wikipedia and npm search engines on or off.

Private by design

WayWayBack is fully client-side and GPL-3.0 licensed: no account, no backend, no analytics, no ads. Everything is stored on your device.

The app talks only to web.archive.org, and to Wikipedia or npm if you leave those optional search engines on.

There is no analytics, no advertising and no account. The source code is public under the GNU GPL v3 or later.

WayWayBack is an independent, unofficial client. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Internet Archive.

FAQ

Is WayWayBack the official Wayback Machine app?

No. It is an independent, unofficial, open-source client for the Internet Archive's public Wayback Machine.

Is WayWayBack free?

Yes. It is free software under the GNU GPL v3 or later, with no ads and no in-app purchases.

Does it collect my data?

No. There is no account, backend or analytics. Stars and history stay on your device.

Which platforms are supported?

Android (APK and F-Droid build), plus native Linux, Windows and macOS builds.

How do I install it?

Download an APK from the latest GitHub release. arm64-v8a suits most phones; universal works everywhere.
